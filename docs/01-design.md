## 1. One-paragraph summary

local_astra is an always-available, fully offline, on-device voice-and-vision assistant inspired by research demos like Google's Project Astra, for a Debian/Ubuntu desktop with a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 (32 GB). You talk to it; it sees your screen and your webcam; it answers in a natural voice within about a second and a half; you can interrupt it; it remembers what it has seen ("where did I leave my glasses", "what was that error I had an hour ago"). The brain is Google's `diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it` (Apache 2.0, text+image+video in, text out, ~700 tok/s on a 5090 at NVFP4) served by vLLM. Because DiffusionGemma has no audio input, speech is a streaming cascade: Kyutai STT in front, Kyutai Pocket TTS on the CPU behind, glued by the *Conductor* — it replaces Kyutai's Unmute handler (design derived from Unmute (MIT), no code copied) and owns the audio devices — which also manages perception, memory and a fast "reflex" lane for barge-in and fillers. No packet ever leaves the machine.

## 2. Goals

G1. **Conversational latency.** From the user's last word to the first audible syllable of the answer: p50 ≤ 1.5 s, p95 ≤ 2.2 s in v1; stretch p50 ≤ 1.2 s with anticipatory prefill and an AR opener. (Astra/Gemini Live-class systems sit around 0.4–0.6 s turn-taking latency, backed by datacenter GPUs and natively-streaming models; we should be honest that 0.4 s is not reachable with a cascade on one consumer GPU.)

G2. **Sees what you see.** Screen (any window or whole display, Wayland-native) and webcam, sampled continuously at low rate and on demand at query time, plus short video clips (≤60 s at 1 fps, which DiffusionGemma accepts natively) for "what just happened" questions.

G3. **Interruptible and socially fluent.** Barge-in stops speech within 300 ms; short user backchannels ("mm-hm", "right") do not stop it; the assistant produces its own backchannels and fillers when a long think is coming, instead of dead air. This is "duplex level L2" as defined in §7.

G4. **Episodic memory.** A local, searchable log of what was on screen / in front of the camera, queryable by natural language with time awareness.

G5. **Fully local and private by construction.** All models, all data, all transport on the loopback interface. Verified by tests that fail if any process opens an outbound socket.

G6. **Test-driven.** Every component has a contract and tests written before implementation; latency budgets are asserted in CI on the target machine.

## 3. Non-goals (v1)

The following are explicitly out of scope for v1 to keep the surface tractable: acting on the desktop (clicking/typing — "computer use") beyond read-only perception; multi-user or remote access (a v2 WebRTC transport is sketched but not built); voice cloning of arbitrary voices; languages beyond English and French (a consequence of Kyutai STT/TTS; alternatives are listed for later); mobile or non-NVIDIA hardware; fine-tuning any model; and "L3" native full-duplex behaviour (see §7). Executing tool calls for real additionally requires the confirmation gate of D16 (T-0107). The tool-result loop (T-0111a, T-0114) does not change this: behind `tool_loop`, on by default whenever tools are on (T-0123, 2026-10-18); tools stay off by default; it returns a call's dry-run description or a search backend's results to the model, which continues; it acts on nothing. *Status of D16 moment 4 (T-0114, 2026-10-17):* implemented behind `tool_loop`, on by default whenever tools are on (T-0123, 2026-10-18); tools stay off by default (docs/02 §4; T-0111a §3). This line sits here because D16's paragraph is pinned verbatim (T-0104). *Status of D16 moment 2 (T-0107, H-T107, 2026-10-19):* the agent is autonomous and the model judges which steps are high-stakes: it asks only before a payment or order, a send on the user's behalf, or a deletion, while routine clicks, typing, Enter and other keys are never confirmed; the conductor adds no judgment of its own and enforces only that a step whose voiced text ends in a question executes nothing (T-0107a); the human accepts that moment 2 rests on the model, and D16's paragraph stays verbatim because it is pinned (T-0104). *Status of computer use (T-0111g, 2026-10-21):* behind `tools = live` (which needs the tool loop), the assistant acts in its own sandboxed browser on Astra's loopback start pages only, while real websites wait on H-T111c-E and acting on the desktop stays out of scope. *Status of real websites (T-0160, James 2026-10-26):* a live session's browser now opens public `https` websites by default, only through the session's egress proxy, with a fresh profile, no passwords or card numbers typed, no downloads or uploads and Chromium's sandbox required; `--no-web` keeps it on the loopback start pages, the injection risk is stated rather than fixed (T-0161 follows), and acting on the desktop stays out of scope. *Status of web search (T-0125, James 2026-10-27):* `google_search` executes against the keyless engines by default in live, and in dry-run with `--search ddgs`; the other tools are unchanged. *Status of clips (T-0182, James 2026-10-28):* the D11 acknowledgement and interim filler clips (`ack`, `hmm`) are off by default (`--filler-clips` or `filler_clips = true` restores them), `still_working` plays at most once per turn and only in a live tool loop that has run 20 s, and the honest-ending clips (`out_of_steps`, `not_finished`, `lost_thread`) play whenever their condition fires; clips are fixed system audio and never enter history. *Status of D16 moment 2 at checkout (T-0192, James 2026-10-29):* in web mode the conductor stops deterministically at a checkout page, refusing every further page action there and ending the turn with the `at_checkout` clip, so the user always finishes a purchase themselves; the agent may still add to cart and attempt CAPTCHAs on its own.

## 4. Users and scenarios

There is one user, James, at his desk, usually wearing a headset but sometimes on speakers. The scenarios below are the acceptance scenarios; each maps to an end-to-end test in `03-test-plan.md`.

S1 *Explain my screen.* "What does this stack trace mean?" while a terminal is visible. Expect the answer to reference the actual exception text, and first audio within budget.

S2 *Follow-up with continuity.* "And how do I fix it?" — must use the previous frame and answer; no re-description of the screen.

S3 *Camera object query.* Holding a component up to the webcam: "what is this and what's it rated for?" Expect grounded answer or an honest "I can't read the marking".

S4 *Episodic recall.* Twenty minutes after a browser tab with a flight confirmation was open: "what time was that flight?" Expect retrieval of the right frame and the correct time.

S5 *Barge-in.* User interrupts mid-sentence with a new question; assistant stops within 300 ms and answers the new question. A user "uh-huh" during the answer must not stop it.

S6 *Long think without dead air.* A question that needs thinking mode or a large screen. Assistant emits an acknowledgement ("let me look…") within 600 ms, then the answer.

S7 *Temporal question.* "What did I just do in the last 30 seconds?" — video-mode prompt built from the frame ring buffer.

S8 *Privacy.* Unplug the network: everything works identically.

S9+ *Think-before-you-speak scenarios*: the tutoring set U1–U10 and desk-work set W1–W10 in `11-tutoring-and-deskwork-scenarios.md` are the demo's centrepiece (coding excluded); each has an eval entry (Q7–Q9) and the tier-B ones a side-by-side timing against Gemma 4 12B (X8).

## 5. Experience principles

The assistant is a peer sitting next to you, not a search box. It should speak in short sentences first and elaborate only if asked, because a diffusion model that commits 256-token canvases at a time rewards brevity in the first block. It should be honest about uncertainty from images (small text, low light). It should never speak over the user for more than the duration of a backchannel. Visual UI is secondary: a small always-on-top HUD showing listening/thinking/speaking state, the live transcript, and a thumbnail of the frame it is looking at (so the user always knows what it can see). Perception is opt-in per source with a visible indicator; the mic mute and Stop sharing are buttons; there is no keyboard shortcut (T-0181, H-T181-4: the mic mute zeroes what the recogniser hears, Stop sharing ends the screen or camera capture).

## 5b. What diffusion buys: think-before-you-speak scenarios

An honest comparison first. For a voice loop the number a user feels is time-to-first-spoken-word, and an autoregressive model streams its first token in tens of milliseconds while DiffusionGemma waits for a whole canvas (~300–600 ms). On short, reflexive questions ("what time is it in Tokyo?") Gemma 4 12B — audio-native, AR, ~7 GB at int4, and at or above DiffusionGemma on most benchmarks without thinking — wins outright. DiffusionGemma's advantage is *throughput after the first block*: ~700 tok/s long-form on the 5090 versus roughly 120–160 tok/s for a 12B dense model at int4 on the same card (bandwidth-bound: ~7 GB of weights per token against ~1.8 TB/s). That ratio, 4–6×, only matters when many tokens must be produced *before* the first spoken word — and that is precisely what thinking is. Table 3 of the tech report shows what thinking is worth to this model: AIME 50.8 → 69.1, GPQA 64.6 → 73.2 in diffusion mode. So the design pitch is not "faster than an AR model"; it is **"the quality of a model that thinks before it answers, with a pause a human will tolerate"**: a 1,500-token thought is ~2 s on DiffusionGemma and ~10–12 s on a 12B AR model; a 4,000-token thought is ~6 s versus ~30 s.

That points at the scenarios to build the demo around — the ones where a person would also pause before answering, so a 2–3 s "let me look at this…" reads as care rather than lag. **The demo centrepiece is tutoring and non-coding desk work** (decided 2026-09-12; coding scenarios T1/T2 stay in the table as the largest usage cluster but are not filmed). `11-tutoring-and-deskwork-scenarios.md` develops twenty concrete scenarios (U1–U10 tutoring, W1–W10 desk work) with the benchmark behind each, the thinking-token tier, and the mitigation for the model's two measured weaknesses. The original list:

**Why this is also where the value is (evidence, 2026-09-12).** The Anthropic Economic Index (six releases Feb 2025–Jun 2026, O*NET taxonomy, dataset `Anthropic/EconomicIndex`) is consistent across releases: the single largest task on both Claude.ai and the API is *modifying software to correct errors* (~6 % of Claude.ai conversations, ~1 in 10 API records); Computer & Mathematical is 34–37 % of consumer use and 44–52 % of API use; Education/tutoring has risen to ~15 %; extended thinking is on in 31 % of conversations and rises with the occupation's wage tercile, and "tokens a given output consumes rise with the estimated value of the work"; 93 % of conversations yield an artifact (explanations 17 %, documents/reports 15 % — 20 % of work conversations). OpenAI's NBER study (Sep 2025) adds writing at 40 % of work messages (two-thirds *editing* existing text) and "making decisions and solving problems" at 14.9 % of work activity; GDPval's economically valued tasks are all file deliverables (1.54 files/task, $391 average); Microsoft's Copilot data shows data analysis with the *lowest* completion rate (< 55 %) — unmet demand. Google reports one in five Gemini Live interactions now involve camera or screen, "popular with DIYers and students for real-time problem-solving", while the camera-assistant literature (VizWiz, BLV diary studies, Ray-Ban Meta) is dominated by "what is this?" and label reading — reflexive tasks where an AR model wins. So the value ranking of the table above is: rows T1 (debugging), T3 (reconciliation/data), T5 (decisions over visible constraints), T8/T11 (document deliverables) and T6 (tutoring) first; camera scenarios only in the troubleshooting/verification form (T6, T7); identification and lookup out of the demo entirely.

**The thinking budget, from latency research.** Human turn gaps are mostly under 200 ms and gaps ≥ 700 ms read as trouble (Stivers et al. 2009; Levinson & Torreira 2015), but assistants that *acknowledge* buy time: with a contextual filler, 2–4 s of delay is rated acceptable and only degrades beyond ~4 s (Shiwa 2008; Boukaram 2021; Maslych 2025, CUI — natural fillers help at 4 s and 6.5 s, artificial spinners do not). At ~700 tok/s that pause holds **~1,500–2,000 thinking tokens**; at a 12B AR model's ~120–160 tok/s it holds ~300–400. That ratio, not time-to-first-token, is what DiffusionGemma contributes to a voice assistant.

Design consequences:

**D11. Thinking is always on for the assistant session** (confirmed 2026-09-12)**.** The chat template puts `<|think|>` at the start of the system turn, so thinking cannot be toggled per request without discarding the prefix cache; and with a 700 tok/s thinker the cost of thinking on an easy question is a short thought (a few hundred tokens, ~0.3–0.5 s) that the model itself calibrates. Always-on is therefore both the cache-friendly choice and the choice that makes T1–T11 work without an intent classifier. The thinking budget is ~2,000 tokens (≈ 3 s at 700 tok/s) before the model must start the spoken answer — enforced by the system prompt and, later, by the length-bounded constraint on the thought channel (`07`); at ~4 s of silence the Conductor speaks an interim update keyed to the source ("still reading the second file…", the GPT-Live pattern) rather than waiting. The acknowledgement clip is *contextual* (source-keyed), because generic fillers measurably do not help. Two facts from the model's own numbers refine this: Table 4's token counts put school-level and instruction-following tasks at ≤ 2,000 tokens (tier A, 1–2 s on the 5090) but graduate-level science, proofs and competition maths at 4,700–6,500 tokens (tier B, 5–9 s) — so tier B gets an interim spoken update and the thinking meter rather than a longer silence, and is where the 12B comparison (25–45 s) is decisive; and on multimodal prompts the model often fails to close its thought channel (report §10; James's own finding), which is why MMMU-Pro looks worse with thinking — the system prompt instructs it to close the thought before answering, the LLM client never voices an unclosed thought, X7 records the closing-tag rate, and a two-state "closing tag required" automaton is the constrained decoder's first production grammar. Whether the model keeps thoughts short on easy screen questions is an experiment (X7: thought-length distribution on the Screen-QA set; if the median exceeds ~400 tokens, a "think briefly unless the question needs it" instruction goes in the system prompt).

**D12a. Pixel-first perception; text assists are optional checkboxes** (revised 2026-09-12 at James's request to showcase raw visual understanding). By default the model sees pixels only: screenshots at the maximum visual-token budget and camera frames. DOM text, PDF text layers, spreadsheet cells and the accessibility tree are *assists*, each behind a visible HUD toggle, all off by default, with a badge showing which are on so recordings are honest. The model's weakest published number (document parsing, OmniDocBench 0.319) is therefore a risk carried deliberately and mitigated by pixel means only: maximum visual tokens for documents, focus crops (whole screen plus a high-resolution crop of the active window or the pointer region, as two images), a model-requested zoom crop if Gemma 4's grounding proves reliable (X13), and the read-back confirmation for handwriting. Q9 measures the assists-on/off delta; the demo runs with them off.

**D12. Two output channels: spoken text and a generated UI** (revised 2026-09-12). Every answer has a spoken part (one to three sentences, voiced by Pocket TTS) and a shown part that is now a **generative-UI surface** rather than a rendered document: the model emits an A2UI v0.9.1 `updateComponents` payload (Google's Apache-2.0 agent-to-UI protocol — flat id-referenced component list, published JSON Schema, Python validator, `@a2ui/lit` renderer in the plain HUD page) using the basic catalog plus our `edu` catalog (Math/KaTeX, Markdown, Table, Chart, Timer, Flashcard, AnnotatedImage, Steps). Quizzes, flashcard decks, MCQ with feedback, step-by-step reveals, worked solutions with maths, comparison tables and forms are therefore *interactive*, and user actions round-trip to the model as events with the surface's data model attached. Validity is achieved by validate-and-repair on commit (fixer → schema → topology → resample), not by constrained decoding (`12` §1.4). A small MCQ is two canvases; a full quiz plus deck is five or six — the burst is the visible form of what diffusion buys.

**D15. The denoising view.** The HUD shows the diffusion process itself: a 16 × 16 token grid per canvas fed by the plugin's passive per-step tap (`states.canvas`, `argmax_canvas`, acceptance mask, entropy), flickering while positions are still noise, lighting up as they are accepted, flashing when a previously accepted token is revised, then settling into the answer text or the A2UI surface. Thought canvases render dimmed under a "thinking" label. It is truthful (the real trajectory, not an effect), it is the first milestone of the plugin (observability before constraints), and it doubles as the debugging view for stuttering loops and unclosed thoughts. A synthesised animation exists only as a labelled fallback when the tap is unavailable.

**D13. The comparison is part of the demo.** The tier-B scenes (U2 physics problem set, U3 proof check, W3 decision memo) run against Gemma 4 12B (int4, AR) with the same perception path, and the HUD shows wall time from end of question to first spoken word and to the complete artifact. On tier-A scenes the 12B is expected to be as good; if DiffusionGemma does not win the tier-B scenes by a clear margin on the 5090, the premise of the project is wrong and it is better to know in Phase 0 (this is experiment X8).

The acknowledgement matters more than usual in these scenarios (sources for this section: Anthropic Economic Index reports Feb/Mar/Sep 2025, Jan/Mar/Jun 2026 and the `Anthropic/EconomicIndex` dataset; OpenAI, *How People Use ChatGPT*, NBER w34255, Sep 2025 and the Aug 2026 follow-up; OpenAI GDPval, Sep 2025; Microsoft Research, *Working with AI*, Jul 2025 and Work Trend Index May 2026; Stivers et al. PNAS 2009; Levinson & Torreira 2015; Roberts & Francis JASA 2013; Shiwa et al. 2008; Boukaram et al. HAI 2021; Maslych et al. CUI 2025; Google Gemini app post Aug 2026; Microsoft Copilot Vision posts May/Jun 2025; VizWiz; the 2024 BLV diary study). With thinking on, the first committed canvas is thought, not answer, so the Conductor knows within ~0.4 s that a think is under way and can play a grounded acknowledgement ("let me look at the log…" keyed to the attached source) while the thought canvases run; the thought stream can be shown on the HUD but is never spoken and never stored in history.

**D16. Speaking while acting (computer use)** (H-T104, the human, 2026-10-16). When the assistant acts on the screen it speaks at four moments and is otherwise quiet. (1) *Start:* one short sentence per request so the user knows what was understood; when the request is a single routine step, the HUD action card is the acknowledgement and the sentence is optional. (2) *Before anything that cannot be undone* (placing an order, paying or transferring money, sending, posting or publishing, deleting beyond undo, discarding unsaved work, creating an account or a booking, changing a password or a security setting) it stops, says what it is about to do with the specifics that matter (item and total, recipient, what will be deleted), asks, and acts only on an explicit yes in a later user turn; the request's own words are not that yes in v1; when unsure whether a step can be undone, it treats it as irreversible. (3) *When stuck or unsure* (two plausible targets, a missing value, a login, an error it cannot get past) it says so in one sentence and asks the user to choose; it does not guess. (4) *At the end* it reports the outcome in one or two sentences, including whether anything irreversible happened. Routine steps (typing, clicking, scrolling, key presses, searches) are not narrated; their progress is shown on the HUD. Confirmations, questions and reports are ordinary committed answer text: no uncommitted text, thought, tool-commit text or text built from a call is ever voiced (T-0080d H4; docs/02 §4 P6), and history holds only what was spoken (H8). This supersedes, for routine steps, the target "a spoken sentence before every tool call" (T-0093 criterion 3's spoken-prefix row; P1/P2 of T-0098, T-0098c and T-0103): the spoken-prefix rate is reported, never gated, and has no lower bound. In v1 (one call per turn, dry run) moments 1–3 are measured by Q13; moment 4 and multi-step narration wait for the multi-step loop. `tools = live` additionally requires the conductor's confirmation gate (T-0107), so that moment 2 never rests on the model alone.

## 6. Key design decisions

Each decision is recorded as an ADR in the architecture doc; this section gives the reasoning at product level.

**D1. Cascade, not speech-to-speech.** DiffusionGemma cannot hear, and no open speech-to-speech model lets you substitute an external text LLM as the brain (MoshiRAG and DuplexOmni are the closest, and both still author the utterance in their own backbone). So: STT → DiffusionGemma → TTS. The cascade is what Kyutai's Unmute already does with Gemma 3; we follow that architecture and its OpenAI-Realtime-style event protocol.

**D2. DiffusionGemma via vLLM ≥ 0.24, NVFP4 checkpoint.** bf16 is ~50 GB and does not fit; FP8 (~26 GB) leaves no room for anything else; NVFP4 (17.5 GiB of safetensors, NVIDIA's `diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it-NVFP4`, experts-only quantisation with attention/router/vision in bf16) is the only configuration that coexists with GPU STT in 32 GB. vLLM (pinned v0.29.0, cu130 container, driver ≥ 580) is the only server today with image input, streaming, prefix caching and Blackwell kernels for this model; llama.cpp support is a draft PR with no server; SGLang is the fallback. Facts verified in source that constrain the design: `--attention-backend TRITON_ATTN` is mandatory on sm_120 (only Triton implements the per-sequence causal flag the model needs); `--max-num-seqs ≤ 4` and in practice 3; the sampler's vocab-sized transient (~2.5 GiB) is not covered by vLLM's memory profiling, so ≥ 3 GiB must stay free on the device outside vLLM's budget; per-request `temperature ≠ 1`, `seed`, `min_p`, `logit_bias`, penalties and structured outputs are rejected (400) — only `top_k`, `top_p`, `max_tokens` and `stop` work; and the chat template emits `<|think|>` as the first token of the system turn, so thinking is decided per session, never toggled per request after speech onset.

**D3. Speak per committed canvas; get latency from four levers that actually exist.** DiffusionGemma is *uniform-state* diffusion: tokens in a canvas can be revised at any denoising step, so intermediate drafts must never reach the TTS. Output is committed per canvas (256 tokens, ~9–17 effective steps with adaptive early exit). Canvas length and step cap are **engine-global** in both vLLM and SGLang (verified in source, see `06-second-pass-risks.md` R2), so the first draft's "small first canvas per request" is not implementable. The levers are: (a) choose the canvas length once by benchmark B1 (128 vs 256); (b) anticipatory prefill (D9); (c) the dual-branch AR opener from the same weights and prefix (`08-dual-branch-decoding.md`) — potentially the strongest lever, but it needs a vLLM patch and the interim is an acknowledgement clip; (d) constraint-driven convergence via a pad-only tail or a length-bounded automaton (`07-constrained-decoding.md`) — a hypothesis, not a measurement: the `diffgemma-fa` campaign ran fixed 48-step canvases and says nothing about steps per canvas, and its accuracy-parity arm was 31 % *slower*. Since a spoken answer fits in one canvas, first-canvas latency *is* answer latency, and the CPU reflex lane (acknowledgement clip keyed to the first-canvas EMA) covers perceived latency in the meantime (D7).

**D4. Kyutai STT on the GPU, Kyutai Pocket TTS on the CPU.** `stt-1b-en_fr` streams words with timestamps 0.5 s behind the audio and exposes pause-prediction heads that Unmute uses for end-of-turn; weights CC-BY-4.0. `tts-1.6b-en_fr` (word-stream input, 5.3 GiB, fixed voice embeddings, some voice packs CC-BY-NC) is the quality tier and fits only if STT moves to the CPU. The default is **Kyutai Pocket TTS** (~100M params, code MIT / weights CC-BY-4.0, runs on CPU at ~6× realtime on an M4 and ~1.4× on a 4-core Xeon — measure on the target CPU — ~200 ms first chunk, EN/FR/DE/ES/PT/IT, voice cloning from a wav permitted): it frees ~5 GB of VRAM at the cost of some quality and a couple of CPU cores. It takes **complete sentences, not a token stream** (`generate_audio_stream(text)` sentence-splits internally; audio streams out). That is fine here, and the first draft's "token-stream TTS" rationale was backwards: a spoken answer arrives as one committed canvas, so the TTS always receives whole sentences anyway. Kokoro-82M (Apache) remains a third option. STT has no CPU-class Kyutai equivalent, so `stt-1b` stays on the GPU by default (run with `batch_size = 1–2` to avoid moshi-server's open idle-CPU-spin bug #390). Its semantic end-of-turn predictor is no longer the justification: TurnBench (Aug 2026) finds Kyutai SVAD and smart-turn v3 statistically identical on end-of-turn (both ~1.0 s median). The `cpu-stt` profile (NVIDIA `nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b` or the newer `nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b` via NeMo-Speech.cpp, Q4_K 0.56 GB, CPU RTF unpublished — measure — plus Silero + smart-turn for endpointing) and `parakeet_realtime_eou_120m-v1` (120M, emits `<EOU>`, p50 160 ms, English) are compared in a one-evening Phase 0 experiment (X2). Every speech component sits behind an interface so Chatterbox/Qwen3-TTS/Voxtral-Realtime can be swapped in for more languages later.

**D5. Derive the Conductor's design from Unmute's handler (MIT), no code copied; own the turn machine as it evolves** (decided 2026-09-12 after review). Unmute's `unmute_handler.py` (~650 lines, MIT) already has the parts a first draft would have re-implemented: pause-probability end-of-turn, interrupt-on-STT-while-speaking, vLLM streaming, word rechunking, and the OpenAI-Realtime-style event protocol. Vision, perception, memory and the prompt layout are additive. What Unmute does not have — DiffusionGemma commit semantics, anticipatory prefill, pause-then-cancel barge-in, the reflex lane, two output channels — is added module by module, and the turn logic migrates into a pure, property-tested `turn_machine.py` the moment we start iterating on turn policy. Alternatives were Pipecat (BSD-2; the escape hatch if STT moves to the CPU) and LiveKit Agents (needs an SFU).

**D6. Perception is sequenced: browser tab first, native daemon second** (decided 2026-09-12). v1 captures the screen and camera from the HUD page itself (`getDisplayMedia`/`getUserMedia` → canvas → JPEG at 1 fps → `astra.frame` over the existing WebSocket): zero native code, one consent prompt per session, identical on KDE/GNOME/X11, and the first "talk to your screen" demo a weekend earlier, at the cost of a page that must stay open, no window titles, no per-app exclusions and no persistent consent. The native path (xdg-desktop-portal ScreenCast → PipeWire → GStreamer on the CPU at ≤ 2 fps, with restore tokens, window metadata via KDE's protocol or a Shell extension, a real ring buffer, and `v4l2src` for the camera) replaces it in M2+ behind the same `PerceptionClient` interface, so nothing upstream changes; it is what makes the assistant a background service. GPU-resident capture is not assumed: the DMA-BUF → `glupload` → `cudaupload` chain falls back through system memory on desktop NVIDIA, and interop is measured (Q6) before anything depends on it.

**D7. Fast lane / slow lane, and the GPU is not touched until you are talking to it.** Following GPT-Live and TML's interaction-model split, the Conductor has a *reflex lane* (CPU-side: a global hotkey or a self-trained openWakeWord model — the bundled models are CC-BY-NC-SA — with a session window so follow-ups need no re-wake, Silero VAD, a turn/backchannel predictor, canned audio clips, a filler generator) that acts within ~100 ms, and a *cognition lane* (DiffusionGemma) that acts within ~1 s. The reflex lane decides *when* to speak, yield, backchannel or fill; the cognition lane decides *what* to say. An owner-voice check (WeSpeaker/3D-Speaker embedding via sherpa-onnx) exists but is a **veto**, not a gate: reliable speaker embeddings need ≥ 1.5–2 s of speech (17 % EER at 0.5 s), so it vetoes the LLM call at end-of-turn and can cancel an in-flight answer, which also covers the "talking to someone else" case. **The Conductor owns audio I/O directly on PipeWire** (48 kHz, 10 ms blocks); the frontend is display-only. That is what makes echo cancellation workable: AEC3 needs a stable reference-to-echo delay, which a browser playback queue cannot give, so AEC runs either in-process (`livekit-rtc`'s `AudioProcessingModule`, the only maintained AEC3 binding for Python) or via PipeWire's `module-echo-cancel` with a dedicated TTS sink, plus a transcript-versus-TTS self-echo filter. The first demo uses a headset and VAD gating with no AEC at all.

**D8. Memory-lite: SQLite + FTS5 with captions written by the brain** (decided 2026-09-12). Every scene change or at most every 5 s, store a thumbnail, timestamp, source and window title (when the source has one); when idle, DiffusionGemma writes a caption-plus-visible-text paragraph for each new episode. Retrieval is FTS5 over captions with a time filter; the top-3 thumbnails are attached so the model re-reads the frames. "Where did I leave my glasses" is the Astra moment and stays in v1; the multimodal search engine (LanceDB, SigLIP 2, Florence-2, RRF) returns only if recall fails on visual concepts captions miss. Function-calling retrieval is v2.

**D9. Anticipatory prefill.** On speech onset (not end), the Conductor snapshots the current frame and sends `[system, history, image]` as a streaming request so vLLM's prefix cache holds the image tokens before the user's text arrives; the final request then only prefills the transcript. Two facts shape the mechanics: prefix caching hashes multimodal content and chains block hashes, so the layout is `[<|think|>?][system][history: spoken text, aged frames → captions][<|turn>user\n<|image|>(frame)] ‖ [memory hits][transcript]` — memory hits (which depend on the query) come *after* the image, thinking is fixed per session, and image-aging happens at `commit_history` so the prefix is frozen before the next onset; and a diffusion model has no "prefill done" signal (nothing streams until the first canvas commits) while the warm-up still denoises a full canvas, so the warm-up stream is kept open and aborted when the final request is sent (computed blocks survive abort). If the attached source's scene changes during the utterance, re-snapshot and re-issue (rate-limited; the code's interval is 1 000 ms); the frames accumulate in the current user message, oldest first, each after the first carrying a positional label, capped at `max_frames_per_turn` (3) with the last slot replaced once the cap is reached and a repeated hash emitting nothing — so every prefill sent during the turn is a byte-prefix of the final request (T-0059; `max_frames_per_turn = 1` is the old 'last snapshot wins').

**D10. screenpipe is optional, not foundational.** It changed to a source-available commercial license in June 2026, uses Tesseract and X11-only capture on Linux, and its OCR-every-few-seconds design is orthogonal to the live loop. It may be run as an optional external memory provider behind the memory interface.

## 7. Duplex levels — what "full duplex" means for this project

Full-duplex systems such as TML-Interaction-Small (May 2026) are single models trained so that timing and content are decided together on ~200 ms micro-turns, with a slower model consulted asynchronously; GPT-Live (OpenAI, Aug 2026) pairs a full-duplex speech model with server-side floor logic over partial transcripts and timing signals, delegating hard questions to GPT-5.5 asynchronously. With a text-only diffusion brain we cannot reproduce that natively, so we define levels. **v1 ships L1 plus fillers** (pause-then-cancel barge-in, acknowledgement clips, the owner-voice veto); the rest of L2 — assistant backchannels, wake word — comes after barge-in works on speakers (decided 2026-09-12).

| Level | Behaviour | Feasible locally with DiffusionGemma? | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Push-to-talk / strict turns | Yes | Cascade |
| L1 | Barge-in: user speech stops assistant speech; user backchannels do not | Yes | AEC (PipeWire WebRTC AEC3), Silero VAD onset, semantic VAD/turn predictor to classify backchannel vs takeover, cancel TTS + abort vLLM request |
| L2 | Assistant backchannels ("mm-hm") while the user speaks; fillers/acknowledgements while thinking; floor offered at natural pauses; no dead air | Yes, engineering only | Reflex lane with a turn/backchannel predictor (MaAI / VAP-style or smart-turn v3 + prosody heuristics) over stereo (mic, TTS-out) audio; a small filler policy; pre-rendered clips in the chosen voice |
| L3 | True overlap: assistant starts an answer while user is still talking, proactive interjections driven by what it sees, speech and listening fused | Not natively. Experimental only | MoshiRAG-style graft: Moshi/`moshika-rl-seamless` as the speaking front-end with DiffusionGemma text injected into its stream. Fits only with NVFP4 + int8 Moshi, and Moshi paraphrases rather than voices the LLM verbatim |

Hard limits worth stating plainly: VRAM (two large models plus speech do not fit comfortably in 32 GB); diffusion first-block latency (a canvas cannot be interrupted mid-denoise and re-used); loudspeaker double-talk under AEC3 (headset-first for anything beyond L1); and the absence of hidden-state access through vLLM, which rules out Freeze-Omni-style "let the LLM decide to interrupt" heads.

## 10. Risks

R1 *First-canvas latency.* Unknown for spoken-length answers on the 5090 (X1); mitigations are the filler clip, canvas 128 (quality A/B in B1) and the AR opener.
R2 *Blackwell software churn.* Pinned container v0.29.0 (cu130), W4A4 kernels via CUTLASS on sm_120, `TRITON_ATTN`, no FP8/NVFP4 KV; boot assertion on the `NvFp4 MoE backend` log line; no package in the venv may expose a `vllm.logits_processors` entry point (it forces the V1 runner, which rejects diffusion models).
R3 *Multi-turn drift.* A 5090 user reported degradation by turn 4–6; the Gemma 4 template re-injected prior thinking into history and caused repetition loops until July 2026. History stores spoken text only, thinking is per-session, the template revision is pinned, turns are capped at ~6 before summarisation.
R4 *AEC on speakers.* AEC3 suppresses near-end speech during double-talk; headset first, then PipeWire echo-cancel with a dedicated TTS sink; in-process AEC3 only once the Conductor owns audio with device timestamps.
R5 *Wayland window metadata.* KDE exposes titles/app-ids through `org_kde_plasma_window_management` or a KWin script (not `ext-foreign-toplevel-list`, which KWin does not implement); GNOME needs a bundled Shell extension; the perception daemon needs a portal app-id for restore tokens and GlobalShortcuts.
R6 *Licensing.* Kyutai STT/TTS weights CC-BY-4.0 (attribution in UI), some Kyutai voices CC-BY-NC, Pocket TTS weights CC-BY-4.0, `moshika-rl-seamless` CC-BY-NC, openWakeWord bundled models CC-BY-NC-SA, MaAI backchannel models unlicensed, VAP models academic-only, LiveKit turn models unusable outside LiveKit, screenpipe source-available; `diffgemma-fa` needs a licence before it becomes a dependency.
R7 *Anticipatory prefill cost.* Each speech onset spends a full canvas denoise (~12 steps) on the GPU; acceptable while the user talks, but the request must be aborted at end-of-turn and the idle captioner aborted at onset, or `max-num-seqs 3` is exhausted.

## 11. Success criteria for v1

All S1–S8 scenarios pass on the target box; latency benchmarks meet §8 p50/p95 for 50 consecutive turns; barge-in *pause* latency ≤ 300 ms p95 (pause-then-cancel: playback pauses on onset, resumes if the speech was a backchannel, cancels if it continues) and false-yield rate on injected backchannels ≤ 10 % (Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 style metrics); VRAM stable over a 2-hour soak (< 500 MB growth); zero outbound packets during a full test run.
