## Goal
`local_astra/conductor/stt_client.py::MoshiSttClient` and
`local_astra/conductor/tts_client.py::PocketTtsClient` stop raising `NotImplementedError` and
become the two real edges of the voice loop. The STT client speaks msgpack over WebSocket to
moshi-server 0.6.4 at `ws://127.0.0.1:8080/api/asr-streaming?auth_id=public_token`: it is a **pure
consumer** of whatever `PipeWireAudioIO.stt_blocks()` (T-0021) hands it — one `{"type": "Audio",
"pcm": [...]}` message per fed block, no resampling, no reframing, no retiming — and it turns the
server's `Word` / `Step` / `Marker` traffic into the `SttEvent` union in arrival order, including
the `Marker` echo that T-0003's `ENDPOINTING` row waits for (`SttFlushed`). The TTS client posts a
committed sentence to Pocket TTS (`POST http://127.0.0.1:8081/tts`, form field `text`) and returns
a `TtsUtterance` whose `audio()` yields 24 kHz mono s16le chunks, whose `cancel()` stops it within
100 ms, and whose `first_audio_latency_ms` is measured rather than assumed. Both are injectable at
the transport (a connection factory for the WS, an `httpx` transport for the HTTP) so the whole of
their framing, mapping, error and cancel behaviour is provable in the fast suite with no service,
no GPU and no socket — which is what lets T-0007's session be planned and implemented against them
now. This implements docs/02 §2.3 and the `SttClient`/`TtsBackend` half of §5, and it replaces the
protocol knowledge currently trapped in `scripts/demo/_listen.py` and `scripts/demo/ask.py` with
tested code.

## Acceptance criteria

Criteria 1–11 are **group A**: proved by the fast suite on any machine with no GPU, no service and
no network, using doubles written by the test-author. Criteria 12–16 are **group B**: proved on the
box with `astra-stt` (CPU) and `pocket-tts serve` up, `gpu`-marked, each run through `/launch`.
Every criterion is checkable before the implementation exists.

### Group A — both clients over doubles (fast suite)

1. **Construction is loopback-only, endpoint-from-config, and opens nothing.** Both clients are
   constructed from a `local_astra.config.Services` (whose validator already refuses a
   non-loopback URL) and expose their transport seam: `MoshiSttClient(services=None, *, connect=…,
   auth_id="public_token")` and `PocketTtsClient(services=None, *, transport=…)`. Constructing
   either opens no socket and issues no request (the `VllmClient()` precedent in
   `tests/contract/test_fakes_conform.py`); the STT connection is opened lazily on the first
   `feed()`. *Negative:* no URL, host, port or token literal appears in the owned modules other
   than as the documented default that mirrors `Services`/`services/moshi/stt-1b-en_fr.toml`, and
   a URL taken from anywhere but `Services`/the explicit argument fails construction.
   (docs/02 §9; `local_astra/config.py`.)
2. **`feed` is a pure consumer: one block in, one `Audio` message out, samples bit-exact.** For
   every `feed(pcm_24k_f32, t_ms)` the client sends exactly one msgpack message
   `{"type": "Audio", "pcm": [...]}` whose sample count is `len(pcm)//4` and whose values are the
   f32le decoding of the input, in order; blocks are never merged, split, padded, re-ordered,
   resampled or re-timestamped, and `t_ms` is kept as the client's own audio clock and never put on
   the wire (the wire carries no timestamps). A byte length that is not a multiple of 4 raises
   `ValueError` before anything reaches the wire — it is a caller bug, not a service failure.
   *Negative (static over the owned files):* no import of `audio_resample`, `audio_capture`,
   `scipy` or any resampler, and no sample buffer that survives a `feed` call. A test feeding
   blocks of 1920 and then 480 samples must see two messages of exactly those lengths.
   (T-0021 criterion 7's ruling, restated: "T-0021 owns the 48 → 24 kHz resample and the 80 ms
   framing; `MoshiSttClient.feed` is a consumer".)
3. **Inbound mapping is total and order-preserving.** Against a scripted double:
   `Word` → `SttWordEvent`, `Step` (`prs`) → `SttStepEvent(pause_heads=…, t_ms=…)`,
   `Marker` → `SttFlushedEvent` (criterion 4), anything else → one `SttErrorEvent` naming the
   unknown type, counted, without ending the stream. `events()` yields in wire-arrival order — the
   flushed event is yielded strictly after every word that preceded its `Marker` on the wire, which
   is the entire point of the marker (docs/02 §4 row 6 "all words ≤ t_eot delivered"). Word
   timestamps come from the server's own field when it supplies one (seconds → ms, documented by
   the census of criterion 12); when it does not, `start_ms` is the client's fed-audio clock at
   receipt and the `estimated_word_times` counter increments. `end_ms == start_ms` when the server
   reports no stop time — **a duration is never invented**. *Negative:* the client never drops,
   re-orders, de-duplicates or rewrites a word (the machine's own insertion sort at
   `turn_machine.py:104` handles late arrivals). (docs/02 §5; docs/03 §5 STT row.)
4. **`Marker` in, `SttFlushed` out — and never from a clock.** `await client.mark(t_eot_ms) -> int`
   sends `{"type": "Marker", "id": <int>}`, remembers `id → t_eot_ms`, and the server's echo yields
   `SttFlushedEvent(t_eot_ms=<the value that marker was requested for>)`. Ids are unique for the
   lifetime of the connection; an echo with an unknown or superseded id is ignored and counted
   (`stale_markers`) — this is the "a Marker left over from an earlier flush" case already pinned
   in `tests/unit/test_turn_edges.py:732`. ***Adversarial, and the criterion this task most needs:***
   with a double that **never** echoes, no `SttFlushedEvent` is ever produced — not after a
   timeout, not on silence, not on `close()`, not after N `Step`s. `scripts/demo/_listen.py`'s
   `DRAIN_S = 2.5` s tail heuristic exists precisely because that spike had no marker; an
   implementation that re-invents it here silently defeats T-0007 criterion 16 while every other
   test stays green. (T-0003 criterion 6.)
5. **Failures are events on the stream, never exceptions out of `feed`.** Connect refusal, a
   handshake rejection, a server-side close, an undecodable msgpack frame and a send timeout each
   produce exactly one `SttErrorEvent` on `events()`; `feed()` returns normally, `events()` does not
   terminate (only `close()` ends it), and queued events already produced are not lost.
   *Negative:* no `except Exception: pass` — a test injects each of the five failures and asserts
   the event; and the error message carries the cause without a stack trace or a body over
   200 characters. (docs/03 I7.)
6. **`close()` is idempotent and cannot hang.** Against a double that never completes the close
   handshake, `close()` returns within 200 ms, `events()` ends, the connection task is not leaked
   (no pending task after the test's event loop settles) and a second `close()` is a no-op. This is
   the measured hang documented in `scripts/demo/_listen.py::deliver` ("closing down reliably
   blocks … cost 10+ seconds of dead air"), which that spike escaped only with `os._exit`.
7. **Reconnect keeps the timeline honest.** After a server-side close the client reconnects at most
   `max_reconnects` times with bounded backoff. Because a new moshi connection restarts the model's
   audio clock at zero, word times from the new connection are re-based onto the session clock using
   the `t_ms` of the first block fed after the reconnect, and the discontinuity is announced as one
   `SttErrorEvent` so the machine can decide what to do with the turn. *Negative:* no unbounded
   reconnect loop, no audio replayed from before the disconnect, and no word emitted with a
   timestamp that silently restarts from 0 — a word attributed to the wrong turn is how history
   acquires text the user did not say (docs/02 §4; `.claude/rules/conductor.md` on attribution).
8. **Backpressure is bounded and never blocks the loop.** With a double that stops reading, `feed`
   returns in ≤ 20 ms (`.claude/rules/conductor.md` reflex-lane budget, asserted against the
   injected clock), the outbound buffer is capped at a documented number of blocks (≥ 2 s of audio),
   overflow is counted and reported as **one** `SttErrorEvent` (not one per block), and the buffer
   length does not grow over 60 s of simulated feeding. The client opens exactly one connection and
   reuses it (`batch_size = 2` leaves two slots for the whole box). *Negative:* no unbounded
   `asyncio.Queue`, no `create_task` per block.
9. **The TTS client sends the sentence it was given, and nothing else.** `speak(utterance_id, text)`
   issues one `POST {tts}/tts` with form field `text` equal to the argument — no splitting, no
   normalisation, no truncation, no trailing punctuation repair (T-0004 owns all of that) — and the
   request is issued inside `speak()` so the server starts rendering before `audio()` is first
   awaited. A `voice` other than `"default"` raises `ValueError`: Pocket TTS 3.1.0's API is
   `POST /tts` with audio out and nothing else (`tests/fixtures/audio/MANIFEST.md`, openapi paths
   `/`, `/health`, `/tts`), so silently speaking the default voice would be a lie.
   *Negative:* `words()` returns **`None`**, always. Pocket TTS reports no word timings; a
   character-rate estimate manufactured inside the client is indistinguishable from a measurement
   to `PipeWireAudioIO.play(..., source=…)` and would let T-0006 criterion 6 cut `played_text` on
   invented boundaries — history would then contain words nobody heard. (See "Spec deltas" below:
   docs/02 §2.2 says the client estimates; `contracts/protocols.py` says the executor does. This
   task follows the protocol and the disagreement goes to the orchestrator.)
10. **The wav is parsed, not assumed, and the audio is chunked.** `audio()` walks the RIFF chunks
    (`fmt ` then `data`, skipping `LIST`/`fact`/anything else), validates the format against
    `TTS_OUTPUT_FORMAT` (24 kHz, mono, s16le) and raises `TtsFormatError` on a mismatch rather than
    yielding audio that would play at the wrong pitch; a `data` size of `0` or `0xFFFFFFFF` (the
    streaming placeholder) means "read to EOF". It yields s16le chunks of at most `chunk_ms`
    (default 80 ms; the last chunk may be short), never emits header bytes, and the concatenation of
    all chunks equals the file's PCM exactly — checked against the committed real rendering
    `tests/fixtures/audio/pocket_tts_sentence.wav` (canonical 44-byte header, 96 960 frames) **and**
    against a synthetic wav carrying a `LIST` chunk before `data` and a bogus `data` size.
    *Adversarial:* an implementation that slices at a fixed offset 44 passes the first fixture and
    fails the second. `first_audio_latency_ms` is `None` until the first chunk is yielded and is
    then the measured milliseconds from `speak()` to that chunk (injected clock in the unit tests).
    A second call to `audio()` raises rather than replaying.
11. **`cancel()` is fast, idempotent and silent afterwards; a failed stream is loud.**
    Against a transport that trickles bytes indefinitely, `cancel()` returns within 100 ms
    (`contracts/protocols.py::TtsUtterance.cancel` contract), closes the response, and `audio()`
    stops cleanly — no further chunk, `StopAsyncIteration`, not an error. Cancel **before** the
    first chunk and cancel **during** header parsing both leave no task or connection leaked and
    deliver no audio (the `FakeLlm.generate` lesson from T-0005 M5: the generator does nothing until
    the first `__anext__`, and that is the window barge-in lands in). Conversely, a mid-stream
    transport failure **after** the first chunk raises `TtsClientError` out of `audio()` and sets
    `utterance.failed`; it never just stops, because a truncated stream that looks complete makes
    `played_text` claim words nobody heard. `speak()` itself raises `TtsClientError` (carrying
    status and ≤ 200 chars of body) on connect failure, non-200 and timeout — the session maps it
    to `contracts.events.TtsError` (T-0007 criterion 13); the exception is deliberately **not**
    named `TtsError`, to keep it distinct from that event class.

### Group B — the real services, on the box, `gpu`-marked, via `/launch`

12. **Wire census: what moshi-server 0.6.4 actually sends.** Feeding the committed
    `pocket_tts_sentence.wav` through the real client to `astra-stt` on CPU, the run records every
    inbound message type with its key set and one example value, and the report states: which of
    `Word` / `Step` / `Marker` / `EndWord` / `Ready` / `Error` appear; whether `Word` carries a
    timestamp field and in what unit; and **whether a client-sent `Marker` is echoed at all**. Only
    `Word` and `Step` have ever been observed on this box (`scripts/demo/_listen.py`); the marker is
    documented in docs/02 §2.3 and assumed by T-0003 but has never been seen here. If the server
    does not echo a marker, that is a finding that goes back to the orchestrator as a spec question
    (T-0003's `ENDPOINTING` row and T-0007 criterion 16 depend on it) — the client still does not
    synthesise one (criterion 4). The census output is pasted in the report and handed to the
    test-author for `tests/fixtures/`; the implementer writes no file under `tests/`.
13. **One utterance transcribed through the container.** Fed at real-time pace with the harness
    supplying the trailing silence the delayed-stream model needs (the client pads nothing —
    T-0021's ruling), the transcript of `"one, two, three, four, five."` matches the fixture text
    after case/punctuation folding on 3 of 3 runs, with the events arriving as
    `SttWordEvent`s in non-decreasing `start_ms` order. The word-lag from a word's audio time to its
    event is **reported with its regime, not gated** (docs/03 §5's 0.5 ± 0.15 s is a GPU-profile
    number; this runs on CPU, contended).
14. **One sentence synthesised and validated, end to end.** Through the real `PocketTtsClient`:
    `POST /tts` returns 200, the body parses as RIFF 24 kHz mono s16le, the decoded PCM is non-silent
    (peak > 0.05 FS), and the concatenated `audio()` chunks equal the body's PCM byte for byte. The
    report states `first_audio_latency_ms`, the chunk count observed before the body completed
    (i.e. **whether the server streams the body at all** — `scripts/demo/ask.py` reads it whole and
    nobody has checked), and the measured realtime factor beside the 6.33× already observed.
    *Negative:* none of these is a threshold here — docs/03 B5 (≤ 350 ms first audio, ≥ 2× realtime,
    two pinned cores under load) is **not** gated by this task and `bench/thresholds.py` is not
    touched.
15. **`cancel()` against the real server, measured.** n = 5 cancels issued at a random point in the
    body, `seed=0` for the cancel offsets: every one returns within **100 ms** and no chunk is
    delivered afterwards. This one *is* gated — it is the contract `contracts/protocols.py` states
    and the barge-in path depends on it (docs/02 §4 `SPEAKING | user_speech_onset | PAUSED`).
16. **Loopback only.** `ss -tunp` filtered to the test process during the run shows sockets to
    `127.0.0.1` only. *Negative criterion:* one non-loopback socket fails the task (docs/02 §9,
    docs/03 E8).

## Implementation notes
Constraints and known landmines; the design is the implementer's.

- **Dependencies: nothing to add.** `msgpack>=1.0`, `websockets>=13`, `httpx>=0.27` and
  `numpy>=1.26` are already in `[project].dependencies` (not an extra). `msgpack` already has an
  `ignore_missing_imports` override for mypy strict; `websockets` ships types. Do **not** touch
  `pyproject.toml` or `uv.lock`.
- **Type the transport seam yourself.** Under `mypy --strict`, take the injected connection as a
  small local `Protocol` (`send`/`recv`/`close`) rather than importing a `websockets` connection
  type into the signature; the real `websockets.connect(...)` result satisfies it structurally. The
  HTTP seam is `httpx.AsyncBaseTransport`, exactly as `VllmClient(transport=…)` does it.
- **The measured msgpack shape.** `_listen.py` packs a Python list of floats, which msgpack encodes
  as **float64**, and the server accepts it — that is the only shape ever verified against this
  server. `use_single_float=True` halves the payload and is plausible but **unverified**; if you use
  it, group B must confirm it and the report must say so. A silent format change here fails as
  "transcribes nothing", which is indistinguishable from ten other faults.
- **Delayed-stream facts, all measured in `scripts/demo/_listen.py`.** Words lag audio by ~0.5 s;
  the model's clock advances only while audio arrives, so a stream that stops dead never emits its
  last words (a 2.3 s clip transcribed to nothing). The client neither pads tail silence nor drains
  nor endpoints — T-0021 owns framing and the session/harness owns tail silence and endpointing.
  Do not carry `DRAIN_S`, `SILENCE_S`, `SILENCE_FRACTION` or the `prs[1] > 0.6` threshold across.
- **`auth_id=public_token`** matches `authorized_ids` in `services/moshi/stt-1b-en_fr.toml`. One
  token, one place; do not invent a second.
- **`batch_size = 2`** (the idle-spin bug #390 workaround) leaves two slots for the whole box: one
  connection per client instance, reused, never one per feed and never one per turn.
- **`Step` arrives every frame** (~80 ms) whether or not anyone is speaking — this is why
  `recv()` never times out and why `while True` over it span forever in the spike. Any "is the
  server alive" logic must be based on something other than silence on the socket.
- **`GET /api/asr-streaming` answers 400, not 404 or 200** (Runs, 2026-09-13): a health check that
  expects 200 will report a healthy server as dead.
- **Pocket TTS returns audio only.** `POST /tts` form `text` → wav; openapi exposes `/`, `/health`,
  `/tts`. The committed rendering carries a canonical 44-byte header, so the LIST-chunk case is
  defensive against a version bump, not a bug seen today — keep it anyway, it costs a `while` loop.
  The service is **not deterministic**: two renderings of the same sentence move every word boundary
  by 20–110 ms (`tests/fixtures/audio/MANIFEST.md`), so no test may compare two live renderings.
- **CPU-only, contended.** STT runs with `ASTRA_STT_CPU=1` because the box has ~4.8 GiB free with
  vLLM resident and STT needs ~2.5 GiB beside a ≥ 3 GiB reserve (docs/02 §7, docs/06 R2
  correction). Decode can be slower than realtime; nothing in the client may assume otherwise.
- **Reflex-lane budget:** no call in either client blocks the event loop for more than 20 ms;
  msgpack packing of a 1920-sample block is well inside that, a `numpy`/`struct` conversion of a
  multi-second buffer is not.
- **Spans:** none are emitted by these modules. `telemetry/spans.py` is not edited; `stt.*`/`tts.*`
  span emission belongs to T-0007's executor, which knows the `turn_id`.
- **Exception naming:** `TtsClientError` / `TtsFormatError` in `tts_client.py`. Do not shadow
  `contracts.events.TtsError`, which is the *event* the session emits.
