# T-0047 — Listening feedback: the page shows that it hears you, and that it heard you stop

**Status:** parked (group A0 landed at 1ea3b27; groups A and B not in implementation; status corrected 2026-10-23 from 'in progress', T-0147 audit: no implementation in flight) · earlier: in progress 2026-09-16 (group A0 only: tests activated at cc2455d, implementer running; groups A and B remain **planned** and blocked on T-0043 group B) · three groups, ordered (A0 → A → B) · blast radius **medium** (one reviewer; escalation triggers in *Blast radius*) · **groups A and B start only after T-0043 group B merges** (`session.py`, `hud_server.py`, `contracts/protocol.py`, `hud/src/protocol.js`, `hud/src/panels.js`, `docs/02-architecture.md`) · group A0 is startable now · origin: the human's first spoken run at the desk, 2026-09-16

## Goal

On the first spoken run the page gave the user nothing to look at between speaking and the answer. STT on CPU runs at RTF ≈ 1.3 (PROGRESS, 2026-09-14 audit), so results appeared ~20 s after the sentence ended — and for those twenty seconds the page showed a static chip and an empty pane, which the human read as "audio is not working" after ten seconds even though it was. Today a page can tell that the microphone is alive only by the answer eventually arriving: nothing on the wire carries the input level, nothing says "the endpointer decided you stopped", and nothing distinguishes "waiting for the recogniser" from "nothing is happening". This task adds a server→client `astra.listen.level` message published from the capture pump at ≤ 10 Hz (a peak-held RMS with a running room-noise floor and a `voiced` verdict, droppable like `astra.turn.state` so it can never backpressure the pump), makes the turn's own phases explicit on the page — LISTENING with the meter live, ENDPOINTING as **"heard you — waiting for the recogniser (N.N s)"** with a counter that keeps moving while the STT backlog drains, THINKING with the meter hidden and the thinking bar running — and adds `state_ms` to `astra.turn.state` so that counter is the server's elapsed time and not a page invention. The latency itself is **not** this task's (that is T-0048); this task makes a twenty-second wait visibly the recogniser's lag rather than silence. Non-goals: no change to what is fed to STT, to endpointing, to the turn machine, to what is spoken or to any latency budget; no VAD, no barge-in (T-0034), no second audio path.

**On the thinking bar (the human's third report, "the bar progressed on the typed path but not on the audio path" — unverified).** The mechanism is one and the same: `Session.turn_state_message` derives `thinking_ms` from `MachineState.llm_start_ms` (set in `turn_machine.py` at the `LlmStart` row, line ~636) and the page extrapolates it with its own 100 ms interval (`main.js` `METER_TICK_MS`, `state.js::thinkingMs`). Four diagnoses, in order of likelihood:

- **D1 (most likely, and not a bug in the meter):** the audio turn spent its twenty seconds in LISTENING/ENDPOINTING, where the meter shows `—` by design, and THINKING lasted a fraction of a second (the typed run measured 0.16–0.40 s in the engine on a warm prefix, PROGRESS Runs 2026-09-15). On the typed path THINKING is the only visible phase, so the bar appeared to work. If D1 holds, the fix is criteria 12–16 (an ENDPOINTING label with a counter), not a change to the thinking meter — and the meter must be *seen* to run, which criterion 11 pins on both paths.
- **D2:** `llm_start_ms` was `None` while the state said THINKING, so `thinking_ms` was `null` and the page showed `—` and 0 %.
- **D3:** the `astra.turn.state` messages were dropped as the droppable kind under a full outbox, so the page had a single stale copy to extrapolate from.
- **D4:** the page's 100 ms interval was starved (1 fps JPEG encode on the main thread while capture was shared).

**Confirmation instrument:** T-0043's session log — `wire.out` records give every `astra.turn.state` with its `thinking_ms` and its drop flag, `machine.step` gives the wall time of each state transition. The orchestrator runs `scripts/demo/session_log_summary.py` on the next spoken session and records which of D1–D4 held in this file's *Rulings*. Criterion 11 makes D2 and D3 impossible to ship undetected from now on, whichever held then.

## Acceptance criteria

**Group A0 — the pure level/floor helper (`local_astra/reflex/level.py`; no shared file, startable today)**

1. `rms_dbfs(samples: np.ndarray) -> float` is pure (no clock, no I/O, no logging; `.claude/rules/conductor.md`, docs/02 §4) and total: digital silence returns the named constant `SILENCE_DBFS = -100.0` exactly — never `-inf`, never `nan` — and a block containing a `nan` or `inf` sample returns `SILENCE_DBFS` with no exception. Hand-computable anchors, asserted to 1e-6: all-`0.5` → `-6.020600 dBFS`; all-`0.1` → `-20.0` exactly; DC `1.0` → `0.0`; a full-scale sine → `-3.010300`. Satisfies docs/03 §3 ("energy relative to noise floor" is a declared reflex-classifier feature).
2. `LevelMeter` is pure and bounded: `observe(rms_db: float, t_ms: float) -> ListenLevel | None` where `t_ms` is the **frame's own capture time** (never a wall clock — docs/02 §4: audio time, not state-entry wall time). It keeps a window of at most `ceil(NOISE_FLOOR_WINDOW_MS / STT_FRAME_MS)` = 38 entries (3000/80); after 100 000 `observe` calls no container has grown beyond that and no other attribute has grown at all.
3. The noise floor is a **running percentile of the window**, not a fixed level and not a global minimum: `noise_floor_dbfs` = the `NOISE_FLOOR_PERCENTILE = 0.2` quantile of the window's frame RMS values over `NOISE_FLOOR_WINDOW_MS = 3000` of frame time. `voiced` is `rms_db > noise_floor_dbfs + VOICED_MARGIN_DB` (9.0 dB). `level` is `clamp((rms_db - noise_floor_dbfs) / LEVEL_SPAN_DB, 0.0, 1.0)` with `LEVEL_SPAN_DB = 30.0`. Every constant is a module-level name whose docstring states its regime and says it is **chosen, not measured**, naming what would measure it (X2 for the end-of-turn constants it sits beside, docs/03 §3/B15 for the energy-vs-noise-floor feature; the caller's ask that "X2/Q5 measure them" is recorded here as owed, not claimed).
4. **Throttle and peak hold.** `observe` returns a value at most once per `PUBLISH_INTERVAL_MS = 100` of *frame* time (with 80 ms STT frames that is 6.25 Hz; the stated bound is ≤ 10 Hz) and `None` otherwise; the `rms_db` it reports is the **maximum** of the frame RMS values since the last emission, so a single loud 80 ms frame between emissions is never invisible. *Negative:* it is not the last frame's value (a "last wins" implementation must fail a named row).
5. **Hypothesis properties** (`docs/02 §4` style, never weakened to pass): (a) `0.0 <= level <= 1.0` and `noise_floor_dbfs` lies between the window's min and max, for any input sequence; (b) emissions are ≥ `PUBLISH_INTERVAL_MS` apart in frame time; (c) **gain invariance** — adding the same k dB to every frame of a sequence leaves every emitted `level` and `voiced` unchanged (the meter is relative to the room, which is the whole point); (d) **a constant input is never `voiced`**, at any level, once the window is full.
6. `observe` + `rms_dbfs` together cost ≤ 50 µs at p95 per 1920-sample frame (regime below). *Negative:* no allocation proportional to the number of frames seen.

**Group A — the wire, the tap, the droppable rule, the state clock**

## Implementation notes

- **Contracts to respect.** docs/02 §4 invariants (nothing voiced that the LLM has not committed; history never holds unplayed text; abort/cancel/flush idempotent) and the §4/§5 spec deltas of T-0006 (drain), T-0028 (a)(b), T-0030, T-0037 (`replay`) and T-0039 (`astra.text`). This task adds **two** spec deltas to §5, written in the same shape: `astra.listen.level` (fields, rate, that it is never replayed) and the amendment of T-0028's "only `astra.turn.state` may be dropped" to the two-kind rule of criterion 10, superseding the older sentence rather than rewriting its history.
- **The pump is sacred.** T-0032 criteria 1–2 are the contract: verbatim bytes, no reframing, no gating, no event per frame, a failure counted and the loop continues. The tap adds arithmetic and one synchronous `offer`, and nothing else. `Outbox.offer` is sync and non-blocking, `HudServer.publish` fans out synchronously, and both run on the same event loop as the pump — that is why no lock and no queue is needed and why an `await` in the tap would be a defect, not an optimisation.
- **Where the RMS is computed.** On the block already materialised for `stt.feed` (`np.ascontiguousarray(frame.samples, dtype="<f4")`), once, reusing that array; do not convert twice and do not copy. 1920 float32 is ~7.7 KiB; `np.sqrt(np.mean(np.square(x)))` on it is single-digit microseconds.
- **The meter is relative, by decision.** The absolute level of a room is not knowable from the mic alone, and `_listen.py` recorded the consequence in prose: "measured in this room, ambient noise peaks around 0.116, so a 0.02 floor meant the timer was refreshed by the room itself". That is the failure this helper exists to avoid; it is also why `rms_dbfs` and `noise_floor_dbfs` both go on the wire — the page can show a relative bar and a debugger can still read the absolute numbers.
- **`state_ms` is the session's, not the machine's.** `turn_machine.py` is sha-pinned and pure; the session already tracks `self._state`, so it records the clock time at which the reported state name last changed and subtracts. Do not add a timestamp field to `MachineState`.
- **Do not route levels through `state.js`.** The page reducer is for turn-scoped state that must survive a reconnect; a level is neither. Keeping it in the panel is what makes criterion 18's early-out correct and keeps `state.js` (T-0037's file) untouched.
- **Known landmines** (`.claude/rules/conductor.md`, `.claude/rules/tests.md`, `.claude/rules/perception.md`): nothing may block the pump for more than ~20 ms; outboxes stay bounded; no clock, logger or uuid in `turn_machine.py` or in `reflex/`; a test that asserts a tautology (e.g. "the message we built holds the number we put in it") is a reviewer mutation target — assert about what the *session* and the *page* did. And the T-0037 mirror landmine, restated as criterion 17 because it is invisible at runtime: `extra="forbid"` on both sides means a one-sided field silently turns live messages into `malformed`.
- **Open ambiguities, listed so nobody resolves one silently.**
  1. **`PUBLISH_INTERVAL_MS = 100`** gives 6.25 Hz with 80 ms frames, not 10. Chosen for an exact relation to the frame grid; if the bar looks steppy to the human, raising the rate is a spec change here (and a re-measurement of criterion 20), not a page-side interpolation dressed up as data.
  2. **Publishing in every turn state** (criterion 9) is decided. The alternative — publishing only while listening — saves a few hundred bytes a second and couples the pump to the machine; it was rejected for the coupling.
  3. **`VOICED_MARGIN_DB = 9.0`, `NOISE_FLOOR_PERCENTILE = 0.2`, `LEVEL_SPAN_DB = 30`** are chosen, not measured. The first live session with a real microphone is the first evidence; record it in *Rulings*, do not tune it quietly.
  4. **Where the panel sits on the page** is the implementer's, constrained only by criteria 15–16 and by not touching `index.html`.
  5. **The ENDPOINTING label wording** ("heard you — waiting for the recogniser (N.N s)") is pinned by a test because the human asked for that meaning; the exact string may change only with the test, in the same PR.
  6. **D1–D4 are not yet decided** (see *Goal*). If the T-0043 log shows D2 or D3 rather than D1, criterion 12 stays as written and the *additional* fix is recorded here as a ruling — the criterion is the fence either way.
  7. **What the meter does with self-speech.** During SPEAKING the STT tap is gated by T-0021, so the level will read near-silence while the assistant talks. That is honest but may look broken; the page hides the meter outside IDLE/LISTENING/ENDPOINTING, which is the mitigation. If the human wants a playback-level meter, that is a separate task and a separate source.

## Regime

No latency claim and no bench threshold is produced by this task; `local_astra/bench/thresholds.py` is untouched (criterion 13).

- **Criteria 1–5 (helper behaviour)** — pure unit rows, no clock, no device; exact arithmetic to 1e-6, n=1 per row; Hypothesis rows at `--hypothesis-seed=0` with the default profile. Frame grid fixed at the production one: 24 kHz mono float32, `STT_BLOCK_SAMPLES = 1920` (80 ms), window 3000 ms = 38 frames.
- **Criterion 6 (helper cost ≤ 50 µs p95)** — this box (`gpu-box`), `git status` clean, load stated in the report, CPU only, no GPU service touched; n = 1000 frames of 1920 float32, seed 0 for the synthetic signal, p95 of `time.perf_counter_ns` deltas, reported with the numpy version. A guard, not a benchmark.
- **Criterion 20 (fast suite ≤ +5 s serial; parallel gate < 60 s)** — command `uv run pytest tests/unit tests/contract -q -m "not gpu"`, serial, n=1 before and n=1 after on the same commit pair, on this box with vLLM/STT/TTS resident and the load quoted; the parallel gate is `make fast-parallel` (`-n 8 --dist loadfile`). Context: serial measured **183 s at 8942d24** (2026-09-14 13:52, 1920 tests) against CLAUDE.md's 120 s ceiling — the ceiling question is the human's open decision (PROGRESS, "Decisions pending the human"), and this task must not make it worse.
- **Criterion 20 (page: ≤ 2 ms per panel tick, frames still sent)** — headless Chrome (`CHROME` from `tests/integration/test_hud_headless.py`), the real `HudServer` + real `Session` over fakes, `performance.now()` over 100 ticks, n=1 run, deadline-not-latency semantics documented in the module docstring (the browser cannot be single-stepped).
- **Any live spoken run the human does with this branch** must quote: profile, model and vLLM versions from `services/versions.lock`, STT device (**CPU, RTF ≈ 1.3** today) and `batch_size`, canvas length, `max_denoising_steps`, thinking on/off, headset vs speakers, the microphone node (T-0045), the assist flags, n and seed. A CPU-STT run's elapsed ENDPOINTING time is the recogniser's backlog, not an end-to-end latency, and must never be entered in a B3 row.

## Blast radius

**Medium — one reviewer**, with named escalation triggers.

Justification: the turn machine, the executor, every audio module, the GPU services and the hooks are zero-diff; the wire change is additive and server→client only; the session change is one synchronous call inside a producer task that already exists; the page change is a new panel behind the existing loader seam plus one guarded line in `main.js`. The audio *path* is read, never altered — criterion 8's byte-identity negatives are what keeps this out of "high".

**Escalate to two reviewers if any of these becomes true during implementation:** (a) the tap acquires an `await`, a task, a thread or a queue; (b) `stt_frames_fed`, `stt_feed_errors` or the bytes fed to `stt.feed` change for any input; (c) `turn_machine.py`, `executor.py`, `audio_capture.py`, `audio_io.py`, `audio_device.py` or `reflex/turn_predictor.py` acquires a diff; (d) the droppable set grows a third kind, or a non-droppable overflow stops closing the socket; (e) group A is dispatched before T-0043 group B has merged (which *Owned files* forbids — if it happens, stop and re-plan).

**The reviewer must specifically mutation-test:** the peak hold (criterion 4), the rising-noise-floor row (A0 adversarial 1), the byte-identity of the fed bytes (criterion 8), the oldest-of-its-own-kind supersede rule (criterion 10), the `llm_start_ms`/turn-state-drop mutations of criterion 12, and the `protocol.js` mirror of `state_ms` (criterion 17 — remove the field table entry and every `astra.turn.state` must become `malformed` in the node row).

- **A1 (group A0 activation, 0733b5f).** Tests written and mutation-proven against a throwaway reference implementation kept outside
  the repo: a frozen floor, a running-minimum floor, a window-minimum floor, a last-frame-wins report, an unbounded window, an absolute
  voiced threshold, a missing throttle and an added `import time` each redden exactly the rows that should see them. Markers removed by
  the orchestrator at this commit; the rest of T-0047 (groups A and B) stays planned and blocked on T-0043 group B.
- **A2 (the first frame emits).** Criterion 4 permits both "emit, then throttle" and "wait one interval first". Ruled: **the first frame
  emits**, matching the task's own "exactly one emission per two frames" and giving the page something to draw on the first block rather
  than 100 ms of nothing. Pinned in one isolated row (`test_the_first_frame_emits`); every other row is convention-agnostic.
- **A3 (`t_ms` under peak hold).** The emitted `t_ms` is the **emitting** frame's capture time, not the time of the frame that held the
  peak. The level is a "what the room is doing now" reading; stamping it with an older frame would put the meter behind the audio it
  reports, and the page extrapolates from it.
- **A4 (quantile convention, deliberately unpinned).** The 0.2 percentile's interpolation rule is the implementer's: the fixture is
  built so all twelve numpy quantile methods, nearest-rank and the (n+1)p rule agree, and the rising-floor row is satisfied by every one
  of them. Do not add a row that pins one convention without a reason to prefer it.
- **A5 (empty block and sub-silence values, unpinned).** Criterion 1 fixes only that digital silence returns `SILENCE_DBFS` exactly.
  Whether `rms_dbfs` clamps everything below −100 dBFS and what an empty block returns are the implementer's; the Hypothesis rows stay
  inside [−97, −3] dB so a clamping implementation is not penalised.
- **A6 (criterion 6 has less headroom than it looks).** Calibration on this box (load average 6.9, numpy 2.4.6, CPU only, n = 1000
  frames of 1920 float32, seed 0): a reference calling `np.quantile` per emission reaches p95 ≈ 26 µs against the 50 µs ceiling, while
  sorting 38 floats reaches ≈ 4.9 µs. The ceiling stands unweakened; the implementer should not call `np.quantile` per frame.
- **A7 (ESCALATED by the reviewer, ruled: substituted silence is not the room).** `audio_capture.py` replaces self-speech-gated blocks
  and xrun holes with exact `np.zeros` on the STT tap, and `SttFrame` carries no flag saying so. Those frames read `SILENCE_DBFS`, and
  with a P20 over 38 entries **eight of them (640 ms) drag the floor to −100**, after which a −60 dBFS room reads `level = 1.0,
  voiced = True`. Measured by the reviewer end to end: after 2 s of assistant speech the meter claims a voiced full bar on an empty room
  for **2320 ms** — at the top of the listening window, the exact moment the human looks at it to decide whether the microphone works.
  That is a worse lie than the frozen bar criterion 15 exists to prevent, and the same failure `_listen.py` recorded in prose.
  **Ruling:** a frame at `SILENCE_DBFS` does not enter the noise-floor window; when the window holds no non-silent frame the floor is
  `SILENCE_DBFS` (so a genuinely dead stream reads `level = 0, voiced = False`, never a full bar). Digital silence is not reachable from
  a real microphone — the module's own docstring puts the 24 kHz dither floor near −80 — so nothing real is discarded. The emission for
  a gated frame itself is unchanged and honest: `rms = −100` against the last real floor gives `level = 0, voiced = False`.
  This is a **task-text gap, not an implementer defect**: criterion 3 as written says "the 0.2 quantile of the window", full stop, and
  the implementation does exactly that. Criterion 3 is hereby amended to carry the exclusion, and it needs its own test row — no existing
  row feeds −100 into the window, and ruling A5 keeps the Hypothesis rows inside [−97, −3].
  **Deferred, for group A:** `−100` on the wire is ambiguous between real silence, a `nan` glitch and gated self-speech. Teaching
  `SttFrame` a `gated` flag is the honest long-term fix and is the right shape for a later task; it is not taken here because criterion
  13 pins `audio_capture.py` to zero diff and widening that is what keeps this task out of "high" blast radius.
- **A8 (peak reset must be pinned — test-author).** Deleting the peak reset leaves all 35 rows and the whole fast suite green. The
  surviving mutant is a session-wide running maximum: the bar never falls and `voiced` sticks True once triggered, which is precisely the
  "live-looking meter on a dead microphone" failure. The suite establishes that the peak survives the gap and not that it is released.
  Criterion 4's "since the last emission" needs the emission **after** the reporting one asserted to fall back.
- **A9 (the cost row does not guard what it says — test-author).** The reviewer measured the unbounded-window mutant at p95 28 µs against
  the 50 µs ceiling, so the row that claims to catch an accidentally quadratic window passes under one. It already computes the
  first-100 and last-100 medians and only prints them; assert them, or move the claim to the boundedness row, which does catch it.
- **A10 (two implementer justifications were measured false; keep the choices).** The nearest-rank quantile is *not* what makes gain
  invariance exact (linear interpolation differs by ~1e-16 with zero voiced flips), and a bounded `deque` does **not** swing by 528 bytes
  (measured delta 0 on this CPython). Both choices stand on the reason that is real: the ring plus `sorted()` costs ~5 µs against
  `np.quantile`'s ~26 µs. Drop the two false claims from the module docstring. Add the precondition the reviewer identified: frame time
  must be non-decreasing, so a re-opened capture stream needs a new `LevelMeter` — reachable in group A, since a stream's clock restarts
  at zero.
- **A11 (correcting my own brief).** I told the test-author to expect the peak-release row red until the A7 fix landed. That was wrong,
  and it said so with evidence: the module **already** releases the peak correctly. A8's finding was that the *mutant survives the
  suite*, not that the code is broken, so the right output is a row that kills the mutant — green today, red under a deleted reset,
  which is exactly what the mutation run shows. A row written red for a defect that does not exist would have been a false alarm waiting
  for the next implementer.
- **A12 (the A9 cost guard was asserted, not softened).** Offered either, the test-author measured the separation first: unmutated the
  last-hundred/first-hundred median ratio is 0.95–1.01, the unbounded-window mutant 4.5–5.7, so a 2× + 3 µs threshold sits between them
  with room for a clock change mid-run. The docstring now says which of the two assertions catches what, and defers the window's *size*
  to the untimed boundedness row as the stronger guard. A claim backed in the row that makes it, rather than a claim removed.
- **A13 (open, needs a decision in group A — not fenced here).** Ruling A10 asks only for a docstring line about non-decreasing frame
  time, so the meter's behaviour on a **rewound** clock stays deliberately unspecified and has no row. Group A is where it becomes
  reachable, because a re-opened capture stream's clock restarts at zero and the session could hand the same meter a smaller `t_ms`.
  Decide there between raising, re-arming, or emitting; the cheap answer is likely that the session constructs a new `LevelMeter` per
  capture stream, which makes the question disappear rather than answering it.
- **A14 (still ambiguous on the wire, deferred with A7).** `−100` cannot be told apart from a `nan` glitch or gated self-speech. A7's
  exclusion fixes the *floor*, not the *reading*. Teaching `SttFrame` a `gated` flag is the honest fix and belongs to a later task; until
  then nothing downstream may infer the cause of a silent frame.
- **A15 (the exclusion is "at or below", ratified).** A7 says "a frame *at* `SILENCE_DBFS`"; the implementation excludes at-or-below.
  Correct: on the real path `rms_dbfs` clamps its return at the constant so the two are identical, but `observe` takes a bare float, and
  under a literal `==` a caller handing `-101.0` would put a value below the module's own floor into the window and drag the floor below
  silence. Not a widening — the boundary the mutation row guards (a genuine −98 dBFS room still sets the floor at −98) is untouched, and
  the 10 dB-band mutation still reddens three rows.
- **A16 (what A7 costs, recorded rather than discovered later).** The window is now "the last 38 **non-silent** frames", so criterion
  5(a)'s literal wording — the floor lies between the window's min and max — is false whenever the criterion's own window contains
  silence. That is the ruling's intent (a gated frame reads an empty bar against the last real floor, not a full one), but two
  consequences are worth having written down: **(a)** after a long assistant answer the floor is the room from *before* it, up to the
  length of that answer old, and refreshes only as real frames refill the window — self-correcting within one 3 s window, and far better
  than reading −100, but not instant; **(b)** a later task that widens the Hypothesis dB range below −100 will redden
  `test_property_level_is_bounded_and_the_floor_lies_inside_the_window` **for a correct implementation**. The real fix for both is the
  deferred `SttFrame.gated` flag of A7, which lets the meter hold its reading across a gated stretch instead of inferring from silence.
- **A17 (correcting ruling A10).** The "a bounded `deque` would swing by 528 bytes" claim was never in `level.py` — it lived in the
  implementer's report, and `grep` confirms the string appears nowhere in the module, at this commit or at the original one. A10 told the
  implementer to remove it from the code; there was nothing to remove, and it correctly declined to invent an edit rather than appear to
  comply. The nearest-rank correction was real and was made.
- **A18 (VERIFIED, and A16 was understated — measured by the verifier).** Group A0 is verified: the 2320 ms lie reproduces exactly
  pre-fix and is **0 ms / 0 frames** post-fix; widening the exclusion by 10 dB reddens exactly the three predicted rows, so the quiet-room
  guarantee is real and mutation-checked; the at-or-below boundary is load-bearing for the bare-float `observe`; the peak-release and
  cost-growth rows each redden under their own mutant and nothing else; per-frame cost p95 6.29 µs against the 50 µs ceiling.
  **But A16 understated the staleness.** The floor is not "stale up to the length of the answer" — it is **completely frozen for the whole
  gated stretch, however long**, because gated frames never touch the ring at all. Measured over a 20 s gate: the floor holds its old
  value exactly, and if the room's ambient level *genuinely changes* during the answer, the page can show a **false `voiced = True` for up
  to ~2.2–3.0 s** after listening resumes. Recovery is bounded by the window (38 real frames, ≈ 3040 ms), not by the gate length, and the
  measured convergence to within 0.5 dB was 2400 ms.
  **Criterion 15's `LISTEN_STALE_MS` guard does not cover this**, because real frames keep arriving — that guard only catches *no traffic*.
  So the page has, today, no way to tell a genuine voice from a stale-floor artefact in exactly the window this task exists to get right.
  It is bounded, self-correcting, and far milder than the unbounded lie it replaced, so it does not block A0. **It does make the deferred
  `SttFrame.gated` flag a named input to group B rather than an open-ended follow-up**: the page must not ship the meter without either
  that flag or an explicit decision to accept the artefact. Recorded as such in PROGRESS.
