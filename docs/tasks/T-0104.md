The approved policy has five parts:

- speak briefly at the start;
- speak before anything that cannot be undone, and ask for confirmation;
- speak when stuck or unsure, asking the user to choose;
- speak at the end to report the outcome;
- otherwise stay quiet on routine steps; on-screen progress is fine.

The human accepted the consequence: "speak a prefix before every tool call" was the wrong target for routine steps, and C3 is dropped.

## Definitions

- **Request.** One user instruction that may need one or more actions. In v1, one user turn is one request and at most one action.
- **Routine step.** An action whose effect the user can undo from the same screen with an ordinary action (back, undo, re-type, scroll back). Routine steps include:
  - typing into a field without submitting;
  - clicking navigation;
  - opening, filtering and selecting;
  - scrolling;
  - searching.
- **Irreversible step** (H-T104 (d)). An action that, once done, cannot be undone by the user or the assistant from the same screen:
  - placing an order, paying, or transferring money;
  - sending, posting, publishing or replying to a message, email, comment or post;
  - deleting anything beyond an undo or the trash;
  - discarding unsaved work;
  - creating an account, a booking or a reservation, or submitting a form to a third party;
  - changing a password or a security or privacy setting.

  When unsure whether a step can be undone, treat it as irreversible (fail closed).
- **Confirmation.** An explicit yes from the user, in a user turn *after* the assistant's spoken question that named the step. The words of the original request are not a confirmation (H-T104 (c)).
- **Ask.** A tools-on turn with no `tool` commit and a voiced answer containing at least one question sentence (a sentence ending in `?` after `drop_special_literals`).
- **Spoken prefix.** As in T-0098 criterion 5 (`_AnswerCleaner.spoken_prefix`, I15). It is logged; under D16 it is *not* a target.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **docs/01 D16.** `docs/01-design.md` §5b ("What diffusion buys…", where D11–D15 and D13 live) gains the paragraph below, verbatim, as the last paragraph of §5b (after D13, which follows D15) and before `## 6`. *Delta (orchestrator, 2026-10-16):* corrected from "§6 … after D15", since D15 sits in §5b.    > **D16. Speaking while acting (computer use)** (H-T104, the human, 2026-10-16). When the assistant acts on the screen it speaks at four moments and is otherwise quiet. (1) *Start:* one short sentence per request so the user knows what was understood; when the request is a single routine step, the HUD action card is the acknowledgement and the sentence is optional. (2) *Before anything that cannot be undone* (placing an order, paying or transferring money, sending, posting or publishing, deleting beyond undo, discarding unsaved work, creating an account or a booking, changing a password or a security setting) it stops, says what it is about to do with the specifics that matter (item and total, recipient, what will be deleted), asks, and acts only on an explicit yes in a later user turn; the request's own words are not that yes in v1; when unsure whether a step can be undone, it treats it as irreversible. (3) *When stuck or unsure* (two plausible targets, a missing value, a login, an error it cannot get past) it says so in one sentence and asks the user to choose; it does not guess. (4) *At the end* it reports the outcome in one or two sentences, including whether anything irreversible happened. Routine steps (typing, clicking, scrolling, key presses, searches) are not narrated; their progress is shown on the HUD. Confirmations, questions and reports are ordinary committed answer text: no uncommitted text, thought, tool-commit text or text built from a call is ever voiced (T-0080d H4; docs/02 §4 P6), and history holds only what was spoken (H8). This supersedes, for routine steps, the target "a spoken sentence before every tool call" (T-0093 criterion 3's spoken-prefix row; P1/P2 of T-0098, T-0098c and T-0103): the spoken-prefix rate is reported, never gated, and has no lower bound.
