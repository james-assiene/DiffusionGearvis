## Goal

The human asked "what do you see?" and the model answered "what do you". Tonight's session log says why, and the two halves are separate defects. First, the silence backstop in `local_astra/reflex/turn_predictor.py` subtracts two different clocks: `SttStepEvent.t_ms` is the *fed-audio* clock ("now", from `MoshiSttClient._audio_clock_ms`) while a word's `end_ms` is *when it was spoken* (`_base_ms` + the server's own `stop_time`). Their difference always contains the recogniser's emission delay — measured at **≈ 510 ms** on the GPU tonight, seconds on the CPU yesterday — so an `EOT_SILENCE_MS` of 800 ms ends the turn after **≈ 290 ms of audio the recogniser has actually looked at**, and a speaker who pauses for a third of a second mid-sentence has the turn closed under them. Second, the words that then arrive late re-open a run, because nothing stops a word whose audio predates the decision from being read as a new onset: tonight the same end-of-turn time was decided twice in each turn, the state oscillated LISTENING → ENDPOINTING → LISTENING → ENDPOINTING, a `mark()` went out per decision, three `stt.flushed` events came back for two real turns, and the churn cost ~0.7 s of turn latency on top of the truncated question. Third, a word whose `EndWord` never arrives is held inside `MoshiSttClient` indefinitely and is published only when the *next* word arrives — tonight 21 seconds later, at the head of the human's next sentence, carrying a start time of 247200 that is not when it was said and an invented 21-second duration. After this task the backstop measures silence the recogniser has examined, in one clock; a word belonging to a closed run never opens a new one; a word the server never closes is published within a bounded hold, marked as an estimate, and can never open a turn on its own. Non-goals: no change to what is fed to the STT server, no feed gate (T-0048 group A), no barge-in (T-0034), no change to the turn machine, to `session.py`, to what is spoken or to any published threshold.

## Implementation notes

- **The recommended rule, and why it needs no estimator.** When word `W` arrives at step-clock time `a`, the recogniser has by definition examined audio up to `a − L` and `W.end_ms = a − L`; therefore the audio examined **after** `W` at any later step `t` is exactly `t − a`. So: record the step clock at which the last word arrived and compare *that* to the current step's `t_ms`. One clock, no delay estimate, no new constant, exact for any `L`, and it degrades correctly on the CPU fallback where `L` is seconds. The alternative — estimating `L` per word as `a − end_ms` and subtracting it — is equivalent but adds state, a cap and a failure mode when the estimate is wrong; prefer the first, and if you take the second, say why in the module docstring.
- **The cost, stated rather than discovered later.** With the correct rule the backstop fires ≈ `L` later in wall time than it does today — for tonight's `L ≈ 510 ms`, an end-of-turn decision at ~1.31 s after the last word instead of ~0.29 s of real evidence. That is the point (0.29 s is not enough evidence to cut a human off), but it puts pressure on B3 (docs/03 §7: last-word→first-audio p50 ≤ 1.5 s). The mitigation is already visible in tonight's data: the pause-head EMA crossed 0.6 at ≈ 994 ms after the last word, i.e. **before** the corrected backstop, so on a healthy stream the heads decide and the backstop is what it was meant to be — the fallback for a server that sends no usable `prs`. Criterion 3 asserts exactly that on the real stream.
- **The endpointer stays pure.** No clock, no I/O, no asyncio (`.claude/rules/conductor.md`). "When the word arrived" is not a wall-clock reading — it is the `t_ms` of the most recent `SttStepEvent`, which the module already sees, in wire order. An implementation that reaches for `time.monotonic()` passes the fast suite on a virtual clock and cuts the user off on the box; that is what the existing purity row exists for.
- **Do not widen the decision tuple.** `Decision` stays `("onset", ms) | ("end_of_turn", ms, p)`; the session logs it verbatim (`stt.decision`, T-0032 criterion 12 / ruling A5) and changing its shape would silently change the log format that this diagnosis depended on.
- **`t_eot_ms` is still the last word's `end_ms`**, never the arrival time: the machine's `FreezeAfter` and the `mark()` that follows are statements about *audio*, and the marker echo is the only thing that may produce a flush (T-0007a ruling A5).
- **The landmine in group B.** Publishing a held word is not free: released during the assistant's turn it becomes an onset, and an onset in SPEAKING is a barge-in through the STT tap — forbidden by T-0032's design ruling and not implemented (T-0034). Criterion 12 is therefore not optional polish; group B must not merge without it.
- **Never invent a duration.** `_close_open_word` today refuses to fabricate an end (`end_ms = start_ms`, marked estimated). The hold keeps that doctrine: publish with the honest flag, count it, and let the endpointer's rules do the rest.
- **What is left of T-0048, explicitly.** (a) Its **device half is now evidenced rather than planned**: with the recogniser on the GPU, word delivery lag was 874–923 ms (session clock; ≈ 490–590 ms in audio time) and the `mark()` → `Marker` echo took **473–482 ms** against **3034 ms** on CPU (2026-09-14 census), with no gap in the 80 ms step stream across a **787 s** session — i.e. no backlog growth at all, which is the thing group A's feed gate was invented to prevent. - **B27 (a negative delivery lag stays signed — the implementer was right not to hide it).** Over a scripted double an `EndWord` can carry a

  clocks is meaningless. On a real server it cannot occur; if one ever appears at the desk, that is the finding.

- **B36 (ruling B29 is WITHDRAWN — the change I ordered is provably inert, and the placement that would honour it reintroduces the bug).**

- **B40 (the late-arrival credit is cleared when a new word opens — bound it, or it recreates the problem it fixes).** The implementer chose a
