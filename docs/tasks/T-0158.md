**Status:** done 2026-10-26, merged 39c7cfb (two reviews, verified at 37d4b4c incl. box check V1: the real `supervisor.stop` stopped a SIGTERM-ignoring probe container in 10.2 s, `logs/t0158-v1-37d4b4c.log`; I2 message + criterion 11 after that, re-checked by the orchestrator; supersedes the T-0130 stub, same bug; first GPU confirmation is the next lean /launch) · **Blast radius:** medium (supervisor; every session ends with `down`, and `up --restart` shares the path; one reviewer, named checks below) · **Groups:** A only (CPU, for v0.1 tonight). The `--init` change is T-0158b, after the release, and needs a /launch boot. · **Branch:** `T-0158-container-stop` · **Origin:** observed 2026-10-26 10:03 in the T-0128c smoke at ffba98c (`logs/t0128c-smoke-ffba98c-run.log`); also seen 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-22 (T-0130).

## Goal

`diffusion_gearvis down` ends with the STT container gone and its GPU memory freed.

Today `supervisor.stop` sends SIGTERM to the launcher pid. That pid is the `docker run --rm --name astra-stt …` front end that `services/stt.sh` exec'd. docker's sig-proxy forwards the signal into the container. Inside, `moshi-server` is PID 1 (`ENTRYPOINT ["moshi-server"]`, no `--init`), and the kernel drops a signal sent to a PID-namespace init that has no handler for it.

The vLLM container stops through the same forwarding because its PID 1, a Python server, has a SIGTERM handler.

Ruling A16 forbids SIGKILL. So `down` reported "still running after 12 s" and exited 1. On 2026-10-26 a third `down`'s SIGTERM made the front end exit. That `down` printed "stopped" and removed the record while `astra-stt` kept running and holding the GPU until a hand `docker stop astra-stt`.

After this task, a record of kind `container` is stopped through the daemon by the container id recorded in it. `docker stop` sends SIGTERM, then SIGKILL inside the container after the grace period. The record is removed only when the daemon says that container is gone or not running.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A container record is stopped through the daemon (the observed bug).**
   - **When:** the record has `kind == "container"` and a `container_id`, and the recorded start time matches the live launcher (T-0033 A11).
   - **The stop:** `stop` runs `docker stop --time <DOCKER_STOP_GRACE_S> <container_id>` with the recorded **id**, never `container_name`. On a permission-denied socket it retries under `sg docker -c`, as `_docker_read` does.
   - **Confirming it is gone:** `stop` then asks `docker inspect <id>` and must get "No such object" or `State.Running == false`, and the launcher pid must exit within `STOP_WAIT_S`.
   - **Only then:** the pid file is removed, the outcome is `stopped=True`, and `down` exits 0. The message names the container id and the launcher pid.
   - **Constants:** `DOCKER_STOP_GRACE_S = 10.0` (docker's default), read at call time. The subprocess timeout is that grace plus `DOCKER_TIMEOUT_S`.
2. **The orphan shape is stopped, not forgotten.** Here the launcher is gone, or its pid was reused (start-time mismatch), but the record is a container record. `stop` asks `docker inspect <id>`:
   - **Running:** stop it as in criterion 1; the record goes only once the daemon says it is gone.
   - **Absent or not running:** remove the file with "stale … container <id> is gone" and exit 0.
   - **In every case:** the pid is never signalled.
3. **"Could not ask" keeps the record (service-lifecycle rule 2).**
   - **When:** `docker stop` or the verifying `inspect` cannot be run, times out, or is denied even under `sg`.
   - **Then:** the outcome is `still_running=True`, the pid file is kept, the message says the daemon could not be asked and quotes why, and `down` exits 1.
   - **Not a failure:** a `docker stop` that answers "No such container" goes on to the verifying `inspect`.
4. **No SIGKILL from the supervisor (A16, restated).** The supervisor never sends SIGKILL to a launcher pid on any path.
   - If the container has stopped but the front end is still alive after `STOP_WAIT_S`, a launcher whose start time matched gets one SIGTERM.
   - If it is still alive after that wait, the outcome is `still_running=True`, the file is kept, and `down` exits 1.
5. **Negative: process records and T-0033 pid files keep today's path byte for byte.**
   - **Covered records:** `kind == "process"` records (today's vLLM) and T-0033 `{pid, argv, start_time}` files.
   - **Behaviour:** they never cause a `docker stop`. They keep SIGTERM, `STOP_WAIT_S`, "still running … not removed", and the A11 reused, stale and exec'd rows.
   - **Tests:** the existing rows in `tests/unit/test_cli_down_status.py` and `tests/unit/test_cli_up_verbs.py` stay green unedited.
6. **Negative: `status`, `up` (without `--restart`) and `read_container` are unchanged.**
   - **Where `stop` may appear:** no path other than `stop` builds a `stop` argv.
   - **Unchanged:** `read_container`/`container_identity` keep their tri-state, and "No such object" is still *unknown* there.
   - **Intended:** `up --restart stt` gets the new stop through `supervisor.stop`, with no `cli.py` change.
7. **Spec change to T-0049 criterion 6 / ruling A15.**
   - **Allowed:** the supervisor's allowed daemon verbs become `inspect` plus exactly one `stop`, in one function, aimed at a container id read from this CLI's own state record.
   - **Still forbidden:** `rm`, `kill`, `ps`, `rename`, `restart`, `exec`, `run`, `start` and `pause`.
   - **Kept, now asserted dynamically:** no code path stops, removes or renames a container it did not record.
8. **Docs say what `down` does.** The `down` paragraph of `services/README.md` (today "There is no `SIGKILL` follow-up …") describes the container stop:
   - by recorded id, through the daemon;
   - after the grace period;
   - with the record kept when the daemon cannot be asked.

   The A17 rows in `tests/unit/test_service_docs_are_true.py` stay green.

## Implementation notes

- **Read the record, not just the pid file.** The container branch needs `read_state_record` (`kind`, `container_id`). T-0033 files return `None` there and keep the old path.
- **Two separate permissions.** The A11 start-time check decides whether the *pid* may be signalled. The recorded container id decides whether the *container* may be stopped.
- **No fallbacks.** Never fall back to the name, to `ps`, or to image ancestry.
- **Daemon answers.** "No such object" from `inspect` and "No such container" from `stop` count as answers only inside the stop path. Match those phrases narrowly; anything else non-zero means "could not ask". Do not change `read_container`.
- **Removal lag.** `--rm` removal can lag `stop`, so poll `inspect` briefly; `Running == false` is enough.
- **`sg` retry.** Keep the `sg docker -c` retry identical in shape to `_docker_read`.
- **Wording.** The process path keeps "signalled pid N; still running after 12 s — not removed". New container failure messages still contain "still running".
- **Docstrings.** Update the ones that claim `inspect` is the only daemon question: `_docker_read`, the `DOCKER_*` constants comment, and `stop`'s A16 paragraph.
- **Constraints.** No new imports from `BANNED_IMPORTS`. mypy strict, ruff. `cli.py` is not edited.
- **T-0158b (after the release):** `--init` in `services/stt.sh`, so tini forwards SIGTERM and STT's `down` drops from about 10 s to under 1 s. Needs a /launch boot.
- **Unverified hypotheses:**
  - moshi-server 0.6.4 registers no SIGTERM handler (to check when a container is up: `SigCgt` bit 15 in `/proc/<State.Pid>/status`);
  - the docker CLI exits on a repeated interrupt.

  The fix must not depend on either.

## Risks

- **Slower `down`:** a `docker stop` that takes the full 10 s grace makes `down` about 10 s slower for STT until T-0158b.
- **Early record release:** "No such object" while the daemon is still removing the container could release the record a moment early. The `Running == false` poll bounds this.
- **Wider fence:** the fence change widens what the supervisor may ask the daemon. Rows 5 and 9 plus the one-function static pin keep "only what we recorded" true.
